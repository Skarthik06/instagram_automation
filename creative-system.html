01 — The read

What the references are really saying

Your six saves share one instinct: restraint reads as premium. The job is to keep that instinct and shed the parts that have become generic-editorial shorthand.

AdoptPrinciples worth keeping

  • Editorial hierarchy — one big idea per frame, tiny labels, oversized numerals used only where order is real.
  • Product-as-still-life — items sit on a calm stage, not a busy collage.
  • Quiet commerce — price as a small chip, discount as a controlled mark, CTA as an outlined pill.
  • Generous negative space — the frame breathes; nothing touches the edges.
  • A repeating frame device that makes every slide recognizably the same author.

AvoidWhat now reads as generic

  • Flat #F4F1EA cream + a serif + terracotta — the single most over-produced "AI editorial" look. We shift the exact hues.
  • Lorem-ipsum body blocks — real copy, real prices, always.
  • Numbered 01/02/03 as decoration — numerals only when the post is a genuine ranking.
  • Uniform rounded cards with one drop shadow stamped everywhere — flattens hierarchy.
  • Stock-model dependence — the feed must hold up on plain product shots too.

Generic if…

Every post is the same layout, same beige, same caption skeleton, mismatched product cut-outs, and a hashtag block copy-pasted post to post.

Distinctive when…

A constant index frame holds, while tint, template and story arc shift with the collection — one author, many moods.

Ownable identity =

The placard + tint system + Instrument-Serif/Space-Mono voice. Recognizable in a 3-tile grid glance, before anyone reads a word.

Why it can't look generic

The frame is constant and the tint is variable. Generic feeds do the opposite — random layouts, one flat colour. Here, a stranger can spot your post in someone else's grid from the placard alone, yet no two collections feel identical.

03 — Multi-product logic

One to seven, never shrunk to fit

The system chooses a layout family by count, so every product keeps a recognizable size and its own price lockup. More products means a different structure — not smaller thumbnails.

1 — The Hero

Full-bleed still, big serif name, single price lockup. Room to breathe.

2 — The Pair

Split stage or "this vs that". Equal weight, two lockups.

3 — The Triptych

One lead + two supports, or an even editorial row of three.

4 — The Quad

Clean 2×2 grid; each cell a full mini-card with price.

5 — Lead + Rail

One hero, a 2×2 support rail. The "top pick + runners-up" shape.

6 — The Contact Sheet

Even 3×2 gallery. Reads as a curated set, not a dump.

7+ — The Index → carousel

A cover contact-sheet, then each product earns its own slide. Never crush 8 into one tile.

The rule that prevents the "screenshot grid" look

Below 4 products → composed single tiles. At 4–6 → structured grids where each cell is still a full mini-card (name + price). At 7+ → the count triggers a carousel so no single item drops below legible size. The system never solves crowding by shrinking; it solves it by changing structure.

06 — Image pipeline

Fix the environment, never the product

The hard rule: the product pixels stay true to source — colour, shape, features, branding untouched. Everything we improve is the stage around it. Every step below is open-source and self-hosted.

01

Normalize

Downscale/upscale every source to one working size & square canvas. Consistent input beats varied sources.

Pillow · OpenCV
02

Upscale + clean

Super-resolution for small/blurry shots; gentle denoise. No feature invention — detail recovery only.

Real-ESRGAN · Upscayl
03

Isolate

Cut the product from its messy background so it can sit on the branded stage.

rembg (U²-Net)
04

Re-stage

Place on the tinted still-life field, add ONE consistent soft shadow + padding. This is what unifies mismatched sources.

Pillow compositing
05

Render frame

Compose the chosen template as HTML, screenshot to a 1080² PNG. Fonts baked in, pixel-perfect, repeatable.

Playwright · HTML→PNG

AllowedEnvironment (design) changes

  • Resolution, sharpness, denoise, straightening.
  • Background removal & replacement with the branded stage.
  • One consistent shadow, padding, crop-to-square.
  • Global light/contrast normalization for consistency.

ForbiddenProduct (truth) changes

  • Recolouring, reshaping, or slimming the item.
  • Adding/removing features, logos, or text on the product.
  • AI "beautify" that hallucinates detail or patterns.
  • Anything that makes it differ from the Amazon source.

Consistency across mismatched sources — the one trick

Don't try to colour-match ten different photos. Instead, isolate every product and re-stage it on the identical tinted field with the identical shadow. Because the environment is now shared, the products read as one set even when the originals had different backgrounds, lighting and crops. The stage is the great equaliser.

07 — Caption engine

Ten angles, one curator's voice

For any collection, the engine can draft from ten stances, then pick the best fit for the products, price story and audience. Examples below are for a real sample set: cargo pants ₹745 (85% off), overshirt ₹599, co-ord ₹829.

01 · Curiosity
Found the cargos everyone keeps asking about in my stories. Yes, they're under ₹800. No, they don't look it.
Use when the hero item is a proven attention-getter.
02 · Conversational
okay so I went looking for one good pair of cargos and left with a whole fit. rating them inside — the ₹599 overshirt is the sleeper.
Everyday feed; builds the "friend who shops well" persona.
03 · Deal-focused
₹4,999 → ₹745. That's the whole caption. Sizes always go first on these, so this is your heads-up, not a hard sell.
Genuine steep discount; let the number carry it.
04 · Lifestyle
Cold-morning uniform: roomy cargos, an overshirt you can throw on, done. Three pieces that quietly do most of the work.
Seasonal / use-case sets; paints the scene.
05 · Editorial
This week's edit is about ease — pieces that look considered but take zero effort. A short list, all under ₹900.
Themed collections; the curated-magazine tone.
06 · Short & punchy
Three pieces. All under ₹900. Save this one.
When the visual is strong enough to lead alone.
07 · Question-driven
Would you wear the cargos or play it safe with the co-ord? Genuinely torn — tell me which and I'll do a fit next.
Engineered for comments; opens a reply loop.
08 · Save-worthy
Bookmark for the next sale scroll: three sub-₹900 basics that don't read cheap. Ratings + links in order below.
Optimises for saves — the algorithm's favourite signal.
09 · Storytelling
Spent a week trying to build one outfit from budget picks. Three survived the wash test and the mirror test. Here they are.
Longer dwell time; a mini-narrative before the picks.
10 · Unexpected
The most expensive thing here is ₹829. I keep forgetting that when I wear it. Good design doesn't check your budget.
A creative angle to break feed monotony.

The reusable framework

Hook (line 1, earns the tap) → Why this set (the through-line: price, theme, or use-case) → The picks (named, with real numbers) → Soft CTA (save / comment / link, one only). Inputs that steer it: product names, count, theme, price band, discount depth, ratings, audience. Rule: rotate the opening structure — never start two consecutive posts the same way.

08 — Brand voice

Sound like a person with taste

Modern, human, a little dry. Confident enough not to hype. The test: could a real curator have written it? If it smells like an ad or an AI, rewrite.

Prices & discounts

Write prices as figures with the real currency (₹745). Show discounts as a transition (₹4,999 → ₹745) or a clean mark (85% off) — only when true. Never round MRP up to inflate savings.

Emoji & punctuation

Zero to two emoji, chosen for meaning not decoration. One exclamation mark per caption at most. Lowercase openings are on-brand; ALL CAPS is not.

Curiosity & saves

Create a gap ("the sleeper pick"), then reward the swipe. Ask one real question for comments. Say "save this" only when the post is genuinely reference-worthy.

Rotation rule

Keep Core fixed; draw the rest from each tier's pool by what's actually in the post, then shuffle the selection. Balance broad (#amazonfinds, big reach) with niche (#minimalwardrobe, real engagement) roughly 40/60. Drop any tag unrelated to the products — relevance beats volume every time.

10 — Quality control

The pre-publish gate

Nothing posts until it clears every box. This is the wall between "curated" and "generic affiliate."

Image

  • Product sharp & clearly visible
  • No AI distortion or invented detail
  • True to the source item
  • Balanced spacing, nothing clipped
  • Every product individually recognizable
  • Consistent stage across all items

Product info

  • Name matches source exactly
  • Price correct
  • MRP & discount correct (when shown)
  • Rating real (when shown)
  • Zero fabricated claims or specs

Visual design

  • Strong first-frame impression
  • Clear hierarchy, one idea per frame
  • Index frame + tint applied correctly
  • Not overcrowded, not boring
  • Doesn't read as AI-generated

Caption

  • Hook earns the first line
  • Human, not promotional
  • Different opener from last post
  • Accurate numbers, one clear CTA
  • Appropriate length, no filler

Hashtags

  • 12–18, relevant to these products
  • Broad/niche balanced
  • Not identical to a recent post
  • No spam or off-topic tags

Brand consistency

  • Recognizable as one author
  • Consistent quality bar
  • Not a generic affiliate look
  • Nothing copied from a reference
